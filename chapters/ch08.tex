\chapter{Proposed numerical formulation and solution procedure}
\label{ch:numerics}

This chapter specifies the discretization and solution procedure for the model of
Chapters~\ref{ch:equations} and~\ref{ch:interface} at the level required for an independent
implementation review. No code is presented and none is yet written (Appendix~B); the specification
is intended to be realisable with standard finite-element components in a general-purpose
environment.

\section{Spatial discretization}
The fluid velocity and pressure are approximated by Taylor--Hood elements (continuous piecewise
quadratic velocity, continuous piecewise linear pressure); the P2--P1 pair originates with Hood and
Taylor \cite{hood1974navier}, and its discrete inf--sup stability for the Stokes limit of
\eqref{eq:momentum} is established in \cite{brezzi1991mixed}; both
temperature fields and the solid displacement use the same quadratic family. The fluid energy
equation \eqref{eq:fluidenergy} is strongly advection-dominated: with
$\mathrm{Pe}=\mathrm{Re}_{D_h}\mathrm{Pr}=348$ the cell P\'eclet number exceeds the Galerkin
oscillation threshold on any mesh affordable at the required near-wall resolution, so the
temperature transport is stabilized by a consistent streamline-upwind (SUPG) term with the
residual-based coefficient
\begin{equation}
\tau_K=\frac{h_K}{2|\bu|_K}\left(\coth\mathrm{Pe}_K-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}_K}\right),
\qquad
\mathrm{Pe}_K=\frac{|\bu|_K h_K}{2\kappa_f},
\qquad \kappa_f=\frac{k_f}{\rho c_p},
\label{eq:supg}
\end{equation}
with $h_K$ the element length in the mean-flow direction and $|\bu|_K$ the element mean speed; $\tau_K$
has units of time and tends to $h_K/(2|\bu|_K)$ for $\mathrm{Pe}_K\gg1$. The temperature field is
quadratic, so the length scale in \eqref{eq:supg} is the \emph{effective} one $h_K\to h_K/p$ with
$p=2$, and the bracket is the classical optimal value for \emph{linear} elements only: for $p=2$ it
is retained as a robust choice, not as an optimum, and the acceptance test is the flux-jump norm
rather than any claim of optimality. The stabilised statement replaces the Galerkin energy equation
\eqref{eq:weaktf} by
\begin{equation}
\rho c_p\,a_T(\bu;T_f,\chi)+a_{kf}(T_f,\chi)
+\sum_{K}\int_{K}\tau_K\big(\bu\cdot\nabla\chi\big)\,
\mathcal R(T_f)\,\mathrm dx
=+\int_{\Ga}k_f\nabla T_f\cdot\bn\,\chi\,\mathrm ds,
\qquad
\mathcal R(T_f)=\rho c_p\,\bu\cdot\nabla T_f-k_f\nabla^2T_f,
\label{eq:supgwf}
\end{equation}
the residual $\mathcal R$ being evaluated element-wise on the discrete solution; the additional term
is consistent (it vanishes for the exact solution) and is applied to the energy equation only, so the
interface cancellation of Section~\ref{sec:coupling} is unaffected. Oscillation-free behaviour of the computed interface flux is
part of the acceptance criteria of test V-B (Chapter~\ref{ch:validation}). The meshes of the
fluid and solid subdomains are constructed \emph{conforming} at the interface: nodes and edges of
the two meshes coincide on $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$. This choice is deliberate: interface quantities are
then transferred by identical nodal values, the interface quadrature is exact to the geometric
approximation order, and no mortar or projection operator -- each of which would introduce its own
consistency error in the conjugate flux condition -- is required. Isoparametric quadratic boundary
edges are used on the deformable boundary and are updated with the same map as the mesh
displacement, so that the geometric approximation of $\Om_f$ stays at the approximation order of
the field variables.

\section{Weak formulation and monolithic interface cancellation}
\label{sec:coupling}
Multiplying by test functions and integrating by parts, the coupled steady problem reads: find
$\bu\in V_g$, $p\in Q$, $T_f\in S_f$, $\bd\in V_s$, $T_s\in S_s$ such that
\begin{align}
&\hat a_f(\bu,\mathbf v)+b(\mathbf v,p)+c(\bu;\bu,\mathbf v)
=\int_{\Ga}\sigma_f(\bu,p)\bn\cdot\mathbf v\,\mathrm ds
+\int_{\Ga_{\mathrm{out}}}\sigma_f\bn\cdot\mathbf v\,\mathrm ds,
\qquad b(\bu,q)=0\ \ \forall\,q, \label{eq:weakf}\\
&\rho c_p\,a_T(\bu;T_f,\chi)+a_{kf}(T_f,\chi)
=+\int_{\Ga}k_f\nabla T_f\cdot\bn\,\chi\,\mathrm ds, \label{eq:weaktf}\\
&\int_{\Om_s^0}\bsig(\bd,\theta):\beps(\boldsymbol\varphi)\,\mathrm dX
-\int_{\Om_s^0}\frac{N_\theta^c}{d}\,\partial_X d_2\,\partial_X\varphi_2\,\mathrm dX
=-\int_{\Ga}\bsig\bn\cdot\boldsymbol\varphi\,\mathrm ds, \label{eq:weaks}\\
&a_{ks}(T_s,\chi)=\int_{\Ga_h^0}q^{''}\chi\,\mathrm ds
-\int_{\Ga}k_s\nabla T_s\cdot\bn\,\chi\,\mathrm ds , \label{eq:weakts}
\end{align}
for all test functions, where
\begin{equation*}
V_g=\{\mathbf v\in[H^1(\Om_f)]^2:\ \mathbf v=\mathbf 0\ \text{on the Dirichlet portions of }\partial\Om_f\},
\qquad Q=L^2(\Om_f),
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
S_f=\{\chi\in H^1(\Om_f):\ \chi=0\ \text{at the inflow }x=-L_e\},\qquad
V_s=\Big\{\boldsymbol\varphi\in[H^1(\Om_s^0)]^2:\ \varphi_2=0\ \text{on the end edges},\
\int_{\Ga_{\mathrm{end}}}\varphi_1\,\mathrm dY=0\Big\},
\qquad S_s=H^1(\Om_s^0),
\end{equation*}
with the traces of these spaces on $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$ in $H^{1/2}(\Ga_{\mathrm{if}})$, the space in
which the shared interface unknowns live. \emph{No pressure gauge is required.} The additive
constant that afflicts a velocity-prescribed Stokes problem is removed here by the outflow condition
B2: because $\mathbf v$ is not divergence-free, $b(\mathbf v,1)=-\int_{\Om_f}\nabla\cdot\mathbf v
\,\mathrm dx=-\int_{\Ga_{\mathrm{out}}}\mathbf v\cdot\bn\,\mathrm ds$ is not zero for every
admissible test function, so constants are \emph{not} in the kernel of $b(\cdot,1)$ and $p$ is
determined absolutely. The natural condition at $\Ga_{\mathrm{out}}$ together with a developed exit
profile then leaves $p\to0$ across that section. Constraining, say, $\int_{\Ga_{\mathrm{out}}}p\,
\mathrm ds=0$ as an extra equation would over-determine the system and, with a Lagrange multiplier,
inject a spurious outlet mass source (register D-25, corrected in D-30). The level that results is reported with every
computed case and is what fixes $\bar p=84$~Pa of Section~\ref{sec:deriv:pressure}.
The second integral of \eqref{eq:weaks} is the geometric-stiffness contribution of the frozen
membrane state, written as a \emph{stress} $N_\theta^c/d$ (force per unit depth divided by thickness,
\si{\pascal}) because \eqref{eq:weaks} is an integral over a two-dimensional domain of unit depth;
equivalently it is a uniform pre-stress $\sigma_0=-N_\theta^c/d$ acting on the reference
configuration. With $N_\theta^c=|N_\theta|>0$ in compression it enters with a minus sign; integrating
across the thickness reproduces the one-dimensional term $N_\theta^c\int_0^L w'\varphi'\,\mathrm dx$
of \eqref{eq:beamcol}, and one-dimensional reduction of \eqref{eq:weaks} gives \eqref{eq:beamcol}
when the transverse load is removed and \eqref{eq:beam} when the membrane force is. The $1/d$ is not
cosmetic: without it the 2-D term would equal $d$ times the intended one, which in SI units is a
factor $3\times10^{-3}$ --- the geometric stiffness would be dropped and the model would fall back
to the linear solid (register D-27).
The bilinear and trilinear forms are
\begin{align*}
\hat a_f(\bu,\mathbf v)&=2\mu\int_{\Om_f}\mathbf D(\bu):\mathbf D(\mathbf v)\,\mathrm dx,
& b(\mathbf v,p)&=-\int_{\Om_f}p\nabla\cdot\mathbf v\,\mathrm dx,\\
c(\bu;\bu,\mathbf v)&=\rho\int_{\Om_f}(\bu\cdot\nabla\bu)\cdot\mathbf v\,\mathrm dx,
& a_T(\bu;T,\chi)&=\int_{\Om_f}(\bu\cdot\nabla T)\,\chi\,\mathrm dx,\\
a_{kf}(T,\chi)&=k_f\int_{\Om_f}\nabla T\cdot\nabla\chi\,\mathrm dx,
& a_{ks}(T,\chi)&=k_s\int_{\Om_s^0}\nabla T\cdot\nabla\chi\,\mathrm dX,
\end{align*}
and $\sigma_f=-p\bI+2\mu\mathbf D(\bu)$ is the fluid Cauchy stress. The forms $\hat a_f$, $b$,
$a_{kf}$ and $a_{ks}$ are bilinear; $c$ and $a_T$ are trilinear and are written with the advecting
velocity in the first (parameter) slot, that velocity being the current state. The Newton
linearization of the solution procedure below therefore applies to $c$ and $a_T$ together. The sign
of each boundary term is fixed by the outward normal of the subdomain concerned: with $\bn_f=\bn$ and
$\bn_s=-\bn$, the two subdomains acquire opposite signs. What follows from that must be read
separately for the two couplings, because they are not the same kind of statement.

\emph{Mechanics.} After integration
by parts, the solid residual contains $+\int_{\Ga}\bsig_s\bn\cdot\boldsymbol\varphi\,\mathrm ds$
(the solid's outward normal on $\Ga$ is $-\bn$), which under the traction equality
\eqref{eq:iftraction} equals $+\int_{\Ga}\bsig_f\bn\cdot\boldsymbol\varphi\,\mathrm ds$, i.e.\ the
virtual work of the fluid traction on the solid. The fluid residual contains the corresponding term
$-\int_{\Ga}\bsig_f\bn\cdot\mathbf v\,\mathrm ds$, but on $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$ the velocity is
prescribed by the no-slip condition \eqref{eq:ifkin}: admissible test functions vanish there, so
this term is identically zero. The two mechanical terms therefore do \emph{not} cancel against one
another. The fluid problem is closed by a Dirichlet condition on $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$, and the solid
equation carries the fluid traction as a computed load; no interface traction is discretized as an
independent unknown because it is evaluated from $(\bu,p)$, not because two nonzero integrals
cancel. The reverse coupling acts through the domain: $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$ moves with $\bd$, so the
fluid residual depends on $\bd$ through the geometry, and it is there that the shape-derivative
terms of the monolithic escalation enter.

\emph{Thermal.} The temperature is not prescribed on $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$. Continuity
\eqref{eq:iftemp} is enforced by a single interface unknown shared by the two subdomains, so one
test function $\chi$ serves both sides and the two residuals are summed for that unknown. The fluid
residual contributes $-\int_{\Ga}k_f\nabla T_f\cdot\bn\,\chi\,\mathrm ds$ and the solid residual
$+\int_{\Ga}k_s\nabla T_s\cdot\bn\,\chi\,\mathrm ds$; under the flux continuity \eqref{eq:ifflux}
their sum vanishes, and the equation for the interface unknown becomes the discrete statement of
normal-flux balance. Here the cancellation is genuine, and it is the reason no separate interface
heat flux is discretized. It depends on the shared unknown: if the two temperatures were carried as
separate unknowns with continuity imposed as an additional constraint, the interface terms would not
cancel and the flux condition would have to be enforced explicitly.

Both statements are exact for a common interface surface. The solid is posed on the reference
configuration and the fluid on the deformed one, so the two boundary integrals are taken over
$\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}^0$ and $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$ respectively; these coincide only to $O(\varepsilon)$
in the largest surface strain, $3.25\times10^{-3}$ at the baseline (Appendix~B, cell A9;
Assumption~A4). The cancellation is accordingly
exact at the level of the model on a common surface, and accurate to that order in the adopted
reference-configuration solid --- it is not an identity of the discrete system. Three conditions are
required for it to survive discretization, and all three are part of the specification: (i) the
single-normal convention of Section~\ref{sec:signs}; (ii) a conforming interface mesh, so that
traction and temperature transfer without projection and the two discrete surfaces coincide; and
(iii) identical quadrature and the same isoparametric representation of the curved P2 interface on
both sides, so that the two boundary integrals are evaluated on the same discrete surface --- this
quadrature-consistency audit is part of tests V-B and V-D. The outflow term in \eqref{eq:weakf} is
the do-nothing natural condition of Section~\ref{sec:bcs} and does not cancel.

\section{Solution procedure for the steady problem}
\label{sec:iteration}
The domain map $\widehat{\mathbf w}$ is defined as the solution of a pseudo-elastic problem on
$\Om_f^0$ with coefficient graded to stiffen small elements near the interface, Dirichlet data
$\widehat{\mathbf w}=\bd$ on $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}^0$ and $\widehat{\mathbf w}=\mathbf 0$ on rigid
boundaries -- the standard solid-based mesh-motion device of deformable-domain computations
\cite{lesoinne1996geometric}. The steady coupled state is obtained by the following fixed-point
iteration on the geometry:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item solve the \emph{solid thermal} subproblem first, with the interface temperature carried from
the previous iterate as a \emph{Dirichlet datum}: $T_s=T_\Gamma^{(k)}$ on $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$, the
prescribed flux $+k_s\nabla T_s\cdot\bn_{\mathrm{out}}=q^{''}$ on $\Ga_h^0$ and zero flux on
$\Ga_{\mathrm{end}}$ and on the remaining faces (B6--B8). The interface flux is then extracted as
$q_\Gamma^{(k)}=+k_s\nabla T_s^{(k)}\cdot\bn$, the heat leaving the solid, with $\bn$ the fluid's
outward normal so that $q_\Gamma^{(k)}$ is exactly the flux entering the fluid in
\eqref{eq:ifflux};
\item solve the fluid subproblem on the current $\Om_f$ by exact Newton linearization of
\eqref{eq:weakf} and \eqref{eq:supgwf} (the advection derivative is linearized analytically), with
the interface flux $q_\Gamma^{(k)}$ imposed as a \emph{Neumann datum},
$k_f\nabla T_f\cdot\bn=q_\Gamma^{(k)}$ on $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$. The fluid temperature level is
anchored by the inflow datum of B1, so this subproblem is uniquely solvable; the pressure-dominated
traction $\bsig_f^{(k)}\bn$ is extracted as the mechanical load and the interface temperature as
$T_\Gamma^{(k+1)}=T_f^{(k)}|_{\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}}$, which closes the thermal loop;
\item solve the thermoelastic subproblem \eqref{eq:weaks}--\eqref{eq:weakts} at the reduced
(frozen-force) von K\'arm\'an level specified by Section~\ref{sec:deriv:beamcolumn} (linear
thermoelasticity augmented by the geometric-stiffness contribution of the current membrane
state; the membrane force is re-evaluated from the computed state after each solve, so the
consistency update \eqref{eq:nupdate} is recovered at the fixed point; one direct solve per
iteration), using the temperature field $T_s^{(k)}$ of step~1. The flux balance is enforced by
alternation, exactly in the fluid subproblem at every iterate and in the solid limit;
\item solve the mesh-extension problem, update $\Om_f$, and recompute all geometry-dependent
data (normals, Jacobians, quadrature);
\item under-relax the interface displacement with a documented factor $\omega$
(tuned once and reported per run);
\item iterate to a smallness of the interface displacement increment,
$\|\bd^{(k+1)}-\bd^{(k)}\|_\infty\le10^{-6}\,w_{\max}$
(achieved values reported per run; the earlier figure $10^{-8}H_0$ sat below achievable
fixed-point accuracy and is superseded), together with residual smallness of all four
subproblem statements.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Why the solid receives the temperature and not the flux.} The assignment of the thermal data
in steps 1--2 is forced by well-posedness, not by convenience. Transferring the conjugate flux instead would give the solid flux data on its
whole boundary: $q^{''}$ on $\Ga_h^0$, zero flux on $\Ga_{\mathrm{end}}$ and the remaining faces,
and the conjugate flux on $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$. A pure-Neumann problem of this kind has the constant
in its kernel, so its solution is determined only up to an additive constant, and it has a solution
at all only if the boundary fluxes sum to zero --- which holds at the fixed point and not at an
intermediate iterate. A flux datum on the solid is therefore doubly inadmissible: the level is free
and the iterate is generally unsolvable. Prescribing the interface \emph{temperature} to the solid
removes both problems at once, because $\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}$ is then a Dirichlet surface and the
remaining flux data determine the field uniquely; the fluid, conversely, keeps one Dirichlet
surface of its own, the inflow plane B1, so it is well posed with the flux as a Neumann datum.
In the \emph{model} the level is fixed by neither side alone: \eqref{eq:iftemp} together with the
inflow datum $T_{\mathrm{in}}$ is what anchors it, and the staggered scheme inherits that anchor
through $T_\Gamma^{(k)}$. Nothing is added to the model, and no reference temperature is invented:
the constant that the Neumann form leaves free is precisely the interface temperature, and it is an
output of the conjugate problem (register D-32). The consequence is worth stating where it bites:
the mean wall temperature $\bar\theta$, and through \eqref{eq:ntheta} the membrane force
$N_\theta$ and the geometric stiffness of \eqref{eq:weaks}, inherit the level from
$T_{\mathrm{in}}$; they are not free parameters of the thermal subproblem.
\emph{Well-posedness is not convergence.} The argument above establishes only that each of the four
subproblems of items~1--4 is uniquely solvable at every iterate; it says nothing about whether the
alternation that couples them converges, and no contraction argument or energy-stability proof of
the staggered map is given or claimed here. The thermal exchange in particular is a
Dirichlet-to-Neumann-to-Dirichlet loop: the solid is driven by the interface temperature and returns
the interface flux, and the fluid does the reverse, so the amplification of an interface-temperature
error from one cycle to the next depends on the relative interface admittances of the two sides and
is not controlled by the well-posedness constants of the subproblems. Convergence is therefore to be
demonstrated numerically, not inferred from solvability. The thermal loop is monitored by the
interface-temperature increment $\Delta T_\Gamma^{(k)}=T_\Gamma^{(k)}-T_\Gamma^{(k-1)}$ and by its
discrete maximum norm
$\|\Delta T_\Gamma^{(k)}\|=\max_{\Ga_{\mathrm{if}}}|T_\Gamma^{(k)}-T_\Gamma^{(k-1)}|$ in kelvin,
evaluated on the interface nodes of the current mesh and reported with each run. Items~5--6 monitor
the mechanical loop through the interface-displacement increment and, through the residuals, all
four subproblems; the thermal loop
is accepted only when $\|\Delta T_\Gamma^{(k)}\|$ falls below a tolerance that is fixed by the
mesh-refinement and iteration study and reported for every configuration, and not before the
successive interface temperatures agree to the precision at which the reported wall quantities are
quoted. No numerical value of that tolerance is asserted here, because none has been demonstrated by
a computation in this work. The interface flux-jump criterion~(ii) of
Section~\ref{sec:quality} is an a-posteriori test on the converged state and does not supply the
missing proof either. A stagnating $\|\Delta T_\Gamma^{(k)}\|$ joins a stagnating geometry increment
as a trigger for the escalation of item~6 to the monolithic path.

The cancellation of \eqref{eq:weaktf}--\eqref{eq:weakts} is a property of the \emph{assembled}
system, in which the interface temperature is a single unknown. The iteration described above solves
the two subproblems separately with transferred data, so within an unconverged iterate the interface
residuals do not cancel and the discrete flux balance is recovered only in the limit; the interface
flux-jump norm of test V-B (Chapter~\ref{ch:validation}) is therefore an acceptance criterion
alongside the residual smallness of item~5. The monolithic path of the next paragraph changes the
solver, not the discretization of the interface unknown.
The escalation trigger to the monolithic path is numeric: escalation occurs when
$N_\theta^c/N_{\mathrm{cr}}>0.5$ at any iterate, or when the geometry increment stagnates
(four consecutive iterations with reduction of the increment by less than a factor of two). At
the baseline ($N_\theta^c/N_{\mathrm{cr}}=0.275$) the fixed point remains the specified path.
If it nevertheless stagnates or shows loss of convergence near the buckling margin,
the recorded escalation is a monolithic Newton solve including consistent shape-derivative terms;
pseudo-transient continuation is the fallback. Linear systems are factorized directly (the
two-dimensional problem size at the target meshes is of order $10^4$--$10^5$ degrees of freedom);
iterative solvers are a scaling option, not a baseline assumption.

\section{Mesh convergence and quality controls}
\label{sec:quality}
Three nested uniformly refined meshes are used per configuration; observed orders of convergence
and grid-convergence indices are reported for the monitored quantities mean Nusselt number, maximum
interface deflection, and pressure drop. A configuration enters the reported set only if the
fine-to-medium difference is within a pre-registered tolerance ($1\%$, tightened to $0.5\%$ for any
headline figure). In addition to the residuals, every computed run must satisfy, and tabulate, the
following conservation and consistency checks, which follow directly from the formulation:
(i) \emph{global enthalpy balance},
$|\int_{\Ga_h}q^{''}\mathrm ds-\dot m'c_p(T_{b,\mathrm{out}}-T_{b,\mathrm{in}})|\le10^{-3}Q'$,
evaluated on the deformed geometry;
(ii) \emph{interface flux-jump norm}, $\|[k\partial_nT]\|_{L^2(\Ga)}/\|q^{''}\|\le10^{-3}$;
(iii) \emph{load-case consistency}: mechanism attribution is performed by separate solutions
(thermal only, traction only, both) rather than by adding fields; exact additivity of the linear
model is verified on a detuned case with $N_\theta^c/N_{\mathrm{cr}}\le0.05$
(Section~\ref{sec:coupling}, Chapter~\ref{ch:validation});
(iv) \emph{mean-stress identity} (\ref{eq:meanstress}a,b) pointwise within interpolation accuracy;
(v) \emph{regime guards} A-1--A-6 of Chapter~\ref{ch:dimensionless} recomputed from the computed
fields.

\section{Post-processing definitions}
The bulk temperature $T_b(x)$ is mass-flux-weighted; the wall heat flux \emph{into the fluid} is
$q_w(x)=+k_f\nabla T_f\cdot\bn|_\Ga$ (with $\bn$ the outward normal of $\Om_f$, so that $q_w>0$ on
the heated wall); the local heat-transfer coefficient and Nusselt number are
\begin{equation}
h(x)=\frac{q_w(x)}{T_w(x)-T_b(x)},\qquad \mathrm{Nu}(x)=\frac{2H_0h(x)}{k_f},
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{Nu}$ singular where $T_w\to T_b$ near the start of the heated segment; that singular
behaviour is documented and plotted rather than suppressed. The areal mean is taken over the heated
length. Pressure drop is measured between area-averaged pressures on the two rigid end sections.
Solid post-processing exports complete Cauchy components (never the mechanical part alone) and
forms $\sigma_m$ from (\ref{eq:meanstress}a) with the identity (\ref{eq:meanstress}b) exercised as
a runtime assertion.

\section{Computational feasibility note}
All prescribed components (Taylor--Hood assembly, conforming interface mesh, direct sparse
factorization, mesh-extension mapping, fixed-point loop) are standard and require no specialised
CFD infrastructure; the dominant implementation risk is not algorithmic but metrological: whether
the checks (i)--(v) are exercised, reported, and used as exclusions, as required in
Chapter~\ref{ch:validation}. The verification of the baseline formulation against an independently
implemented model (an open-source finite-element platform) is part of the validation methodology
(Section~\ref{sec:replication}).
