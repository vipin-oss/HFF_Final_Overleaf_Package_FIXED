# Cover letter

**To:** The Editor-in-Chief
*International Journal of Numerical Methods for Heat & Fluid Flow*

**Manuscript:** *Thermally induced self-deformation of a heated channel wall: a coupled
conjugate flow, heat-transfer and thermoelastic analysis*

**Type:** Research paper

Dear Editor,

I submit the enclosed manuscript for consideration as a research paper in *HFF*. It treats a
configuration that is common in compact heat exchangers and thin-walled instrumented test
sections and that the literature has not closed: a narrow heated passage whose upper wall is a
thin, axially restrained strip, heated on its outer face and free to bow into the passage. The
wall temperature that drives the bow is itself set by the convective resistance of the passage
whose height the bow controls, so the loop has no natural truncation.

**What is new, and why it is not another parametric study.**

1. *The loop is closed in closed form.* Plane-strain thermoelasticity is reduced to an
   axially restrained beam–column strip by a variational principle; the axial-compatibility
   map is proved a contraction ($q=0.757$ at the design point), and the thermal closure of the
   wall strip is proved exact for the horizontal mean. The hydraulic resistance of the bowed
   passage is a Gauss hypergeometric function of the bow ratio, generalised here to
   self-similar shapes, with an a priori truncation bound and a stability lemma in the shape.
2. *The hydraulic feedback collapses onto one curve.* A single shape integral, evaluated once
   on the design-point profile and containing no fitted coefficient, reproduces the strip
   resistance of all twenty-four converged coupled runs of five independent parameter sweeps to
   0.40 % (mean 0.11 %), against 19.7 % for the kinematic $\sin^2$ law. This converts the design
   rule from a formula checked at a point into a chart with a measured, family-wide error bar —
   the point on which single-point studies are usually criticised.
3. *Every number is computed twice.* The reduced model is verified against an independent
   coupled finite-element solution on twenty-four parameter sets, and the solver is verified
   against classical analytical solutions (plane-channel Nusselt number to 0.5 %, the
   closed-form beam–column solution to $2.5\times10^{-8}$, an exact Neumann wall to
   $5\times10^{-14}$), with a Grid Convergence Index on five quantities of interest.

The paper also states where the model stops: the small-slope ceiling is crossed at
$5.10\ \mathrm{kW/m^2}$, before the strength and stability limits, and radiation — the largest
unmodelled effect at 9 % of the imposed flux — is quantified rather than ignored.

**Fit with the journal.** The readership of *HFF* works on exactly this class of problem:
conjugate heat transfer in channels with walls that are thick enough to conduct and thin enough
to matter. The manuscript gives such workers a design chart, the conditions under which the
one-dimensional wall closure is adequate, and a verification record that can be reproduced from
the accompanying program in about twelve minutes.

**Statements.** The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere and is not under
consideration by another journal. The author declares no conflict of interest and no specific
funding. All numerical results, the complete source, the test suite and the machine-readable
outputs are included with the submission; every figure and table is generated from those
outputs, and no number in the text is typed by hand.
